0. Three-Year Financial Trend Dashboard

0.1 Revenue & Profitability Trajectory

DemoMotor delivered robust revenue growth across the three-year period, expanding from RMB137.3 billion in FY2022 to RMB173.2 billion in FY2023 (26.1% YoY growth) and further to RMB202.2 billion in FY2024 (16.7% YoY growth). While the growth rate decelerated, the absolute increase remained substantial, adding nearly RMB65 billion in revenue over two years. Gross margin remained remarkably stable, hovering around 19% throughout the period — 19.37% in FY2022, dipping slightly to 18.73% in FY2023, and recovering to 19.51% in FY2024. This stability suggests effective cost management despite rising raw material costs and competitive pricing pressures in the automotive industry.

The revenue growth was driven by both domestic and overseas expansion. Overseas revenue surged from RMB26.4 billion in FY2022 (19.2% of total) to RMB53.1 billion in FY2023 (30.7%) and RMB81.1 billion in FY2024 (40.1%), reflecting the company’s successful globalization strategy. New energy vehicle sales also accelerated, reaching 322,200 units in FY2024, up 25.65% year-on-year, with a domestic passenger vehicle penetration rate of 43.99%.

0.2 Working Capital Efficiency Evolution

The company’s working capital efficiency improved meaningfully over the three years. DSO declined steadily from 15.99 days in FY2022 to 14.54 days in FY2023 and 13.06 days in FY2024, indicating faster collection from customers — a sign of strong buyer bargaining power and efficient receivables management. DIO fluctuated: 59.89 days in FY2022, increasing to 63.53 days in FY2023 (likely due to inventory build-up for new model launches), then declining to 58.35 days in FY2024 as production and sales aligned.

DPO showed the most notable shift, decreasing sharply from 109.41 days in FY2022 to 90.56 days in FY2023, then slightly increasing to 94.30 days in FY2024. The reduction in DPO suggests the company is paying suppliers faster, possibly to secure better terms or support supplier relationships during global expansion. Despite this, the CCC remained negative throughout the period: -33.53 days in FY2022, -12.49 days in FY2023, and -22.89 days in FY2024. The negative CCC confirms that DemoMotor operates with a structural working capital surplus — it collects cash from customers well before paying suppliers, a hallmark of dominant market positioning.

0.3 Strategic Consistency & Execution Alignment

The company’s strategic narrative remained highly consistent across all three years, centered on globalization and new energy transformation. In FY2022, the strategy emphasized a “forest-like ecological system” and brand diversification (Haval, WEY, ORA, TANK, DemoMotor Pickup). By FY2023, the strategy evolved to “ONE DemoMotor” with full market coverage in ASEAN and GCC regions. In FY2024, the strategy deepened to “ecosystem globalization” with localized production in Thailand and Brazil, and new CKD assembly in Malaysia and Vietnam, targeting 1 million overseas sales by 2030. No material inconsistencies were detected between financial results and strategic claims — overseas revenue growth and new energy vehicle penetration consistently supported the stated strategy. Policy consistency could not be fully assessed from the available structured data — manual review of the full notes is recommended.

1. Wholesale Banking Strategic Advisory Proposition

1.1 Commercial Alignment & Value Thesis

DemoMotor is an ideal client for a wholesale banking division due to its aggressive ASEAN expansion, strong working capital profile, and growing need for cross-border financial solutions. The company’s strategic pivot toward globalization — particularly in Thailand, Malaysia, and Vietnam — aligns directly with the bank’s core ASEAN network and cross-border banking expertise. With overseas revenue now representing 40% of total revenue and a target of 1 million overseas sales by 2030, DemoMotor requires a banking partner that can provide seamless cash management, trade finance, and advisory services across multiple ASEAN markets.

The company’s negative working capital position (CCC of -22.89 days) indicates strong operational efficiency, but as it scales globally, it will face new complexities: multi-currency cash flows, fragmented supplier bases, and regulatory differences across jurisdictions. The bank’s digital treasury platform, with single-login access across 10 ASEAN+GCN markets, is directly applicable to streamline the company’s treasury operations. Additionally, the company’s high gearing ratio (193.79% in FY2023) and significant capex requirements create a clear lending opportunity, particularly for Sustainability-Linked Loans tied to its new energy transition.

1.2 Priority Banking Products & Solutions Map

Given the company’s DPO of 94.30 days and a global supplier base of over 500 ecosystem partners, the bank’s Supplier Finance solution is directly applicable. By extending financing to the company’s key suppliers, the bank can help Great Wall Motor optimize its payment terms while supporting supply chain resilience. Similarly, Distributor Finance can support the company’s overseas dealer network, particularly in fast-growing ASEAN markets where local distributors may lack working capital.

The company’s multi-entity structure in Thailand (seven subsidiaries) and planned expansion into Malaysia and Vietnam creates a clear need for Notional Pooling and Cross-Border Cash Sweep. These solutions would allow DemoMotor to centralize liquidity across its ASEAN entities, reduce idle cash, and optimize interest income. the bank's digital treasury platform would provide the digital backbone for this regional treasury structure, offering real-time visibility and control.

The company’s ambitious new energy vehicle strategy — with R&D spending up 43.56% in FY2022 and EV sales growing 25.65% in FY2024 — makes it a strong candidate for Sustainability-Linked Loans. The bank can structure a facility with KPIs tied to EV sales mix, manufacturing emissions reduction, or supply chain sustainability, aligning the company’s financing costs with its decarbonization trajectory.

As the company enters Malaysia and Vietnam through CKD assembly, FDI Advisory services are directly relevant. The bank’s track record of assisting thousands of companies with cross-border expansion, including SGD 55+ billion in projected investments, can help DemoMotor navigate regulatory requirements, local financing, and partner selection in these new markets.

1.3 ASEAN Cross-Border & FDI Advisory Scopes

DemoMotor’s ASEAN footprint is concentrated in Thailand, where it operates a full manufacturing base and seven subsidiaries covering engine manufacturing, parts production, and sales. In FY2024, the company expanded into Malaysia (first CKD assembly in ASEAN) and Vietnam (CKD cooperation), signaling a deliberate strategy to build localized production across the region. This creates multiple cross-border banking opportunities.

First, the company has intra-group trade flows between its China headquarters and Thailand manufacturing base, as well as between Thailand and new CKD plants in Malaysia and Vietnam. The bank’s Cross-Border Trade Finance — including Import/Export LC and Invoice Financing — can support these flows efficiently. Second, the company’s multi-entity structure in Thailand and planned entities in Malaysia and Vietnam require regional cash management. The bank’s Notional Pooling across Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, and Vietnam would allow the company to centralize liquidity and reduce FX conversion costs. Third, as the company establishes operations in Malaysia and Vietnam, the bank’s FDI Advisory can provide market entry support, including local regulatory guidance, financing for plant construction, and introductions to local partners.

3. Capital Efficiency & Supply Chain Gravity

3.1 Cash Cycle Velocity Analysis

In FY2024, DemoMotor’s working capital metrics demonstrate exceptional efficiency. DSO of 13.06 days indicates that the company collects payment from customers in less than two weeks — a reflection of strong brand power and disciplined credit management. DIO of 58.35 days is reasonable for an automotive manufacturer, balancing the need to maintain inventory for a diverse product lineup (SUVs, sedans, pickups, and new energy vehicles) with efficient production planning. DPO of 94.30 days shows that the company takes over three months to pay suppliers, leveraging its scale and bargaining power.

The resulting CCC of -22.89 days means the company receives cash from customers nearly 23 days before it must pay its suppliers. This negative working capital position is a structural advantage, effectively providing the company with free financing from its supply chain. Compared to FY2022 (CCC of -33.53 days), the position has tightened slightly, primarily due to the reduction in DPO from 109.41 days to 94.30 days. However, the position remains strongly favorable and provides a buffer for the company’s capex-intensive expansion.

3.2 Supplier & Buyer Bargaining Dynamics

DemoMotor’s working capital dynamics reveal significant bargaining power over both suppliers and buyers. With DPO of 94.30 days versus DSO of 13.06 days, the company extracts 81 days of net financing from its supply chain. This is sustainable as long as the company maintains its growth trajectory and market position. The slight reduction in DPO from FY2022 levels may indicate that the company is voluntarily paying suppliers faster to secure priority access to components during global expansion, or to support supplier relationships in new markets.

The company’s buyer bargaining power is equally impressive — DSO of 13.06 days is among the best in the automotive industry, suggesting that dealers and customers pay promptly, likely due to strong demand for the company’s products and disciplined credit terms. The negative working capital position is verified: Accounts Payable of RMB43.5 billion exceeds Accounts Receivable of RMB7.3 billion plus Inventory of RMB25.4 billion (total RMB32.7 billion), confirming the structural surplus.

4.2 Ecosystem Moat & Risk Isolation

DemoMotor’s corporate structure reveals a well-organized global ecosystem with clear functional specialization. The Thailand cluster — centered on DemoMotor Manufacturing (Thailand) Co., Ltd. — serves as the ASEAN manufacturing and sales hub, with indirect subsidiaries covering engine, parts, and systems production. This vertical integration within Thailand provides operational ring-fencing: the Thai entities operate as a self-contained manufacturing ecosystem, reducing direct exposure of the listed parent to local operational risks.

The Hong Kong entities (Tide Technology, HWA Ding, Yixin Development) function as centralized investment and financing platforms, suggesting that the company manages its offshore capital structure through Hong Kong. This provides a degree of risk isolation for cross-border financing activities. The European holding structure (NOBO entities in Netherlands and Germany) similarly centralizes European operations, while the Brazilian and Russian subsidiaries operate as standalone manufacturing bases.

However, the structure also creates concentration risk: Thailand is the sole ASEAN manufacturing base, and any disruption there (political, regulatory, or supply chain) would directly impact the company’s ASEAN expansion plans. The parent company likely provides guarantees for its major subsidiaries, though this is not explicitly disclosed in the available data. Overall, the corporate structure provides adequate operational ring-fencing but limited legal isolation, as the listed parent is ultimately exposed to all subsidiaries’ performance.

5. Strategic Business Drivers & Forward Outlook

5.1 Primary Revenue Engines

DemoMotor’s revenue is driven by two primary streams: automobile sales ( RMB175.5 billion in FY2024, 86.8% of total revenue) and automotive parts and components ( RMB26.7 billion, 13.2% ). The company’s brand portfolio — Haval, WEY, TANK, DemoMotor Pickup, ORA, and DemoMotor SOUO — covers SUVs, sedans, pickups, and MPVs, with a growing focus on new energy vehicles. Overseas revenue has become a critical growth engine, rising from 19.2% of total in FY2022 to 40.1% in FY2024, with key markets including Thailand, Australia, South Africa, Russia, and Saudi Arabia.

New energy vehicle sales are a key margin expansion vector. In FY2024, the company sold 322,200 new energy vehicles, up 25.65% year-on-year, with a domestic passenger vehicle penetration rate of 43.99%. The company’s investment in hybrid architectures (Hi4, Hi4-T, Hi4-Z) and pure electric platforms positions it to capture the growing EV demand in both domestic and overseas markets. ASEAN revenue contribution is not explicitly disclosed, but given the Thailand manufacturing base and new CKD operations in Malaysia and Vietnam, ASEAN is expected to become a material revenue contributor over the next 3-5 years.

5.2 Forward-Looking Execution Risks

DemoMotor faces several execution risks that could impact its ASEAN expansion and overall growth trajectory. First, changes in the international trade environment — including tariffs, trade barriers, and geopolitical tensions — could disrupt the company’s export strategy, particularly in key markets like the EU and ASEAN. Second, the rapid development of intelligent connected and new energy technologies requires continuous R&D investment to maintain competitiveness; the company’s R&D spending increased 43.56% in FY2022, and this pace is likely to continue, pressuring margins.

Third, AI impact on business models and competition is explicitly identified as a risk, as artificial intelligence could reshape automotive manufacturing, autonomous driving, and customer engagement. Fourth, ASEAN-specific risks include regulatory differences across markets, local competition from established Japanese and Korean automakers, and supply chain disruptions in emerging markets. The company’s mitigation measures — consolidating the domestic market, accelerating internationalization, full transition to new energy, and developing full-stack self-research — are credible but execution-dependent.

8. Deep-Dive Strategic Implications & Advisory Scope

8.1 Working Capital Intervention Scopes

DemoMotor’s negative working capital position (CCC of -22.89 days) is already highly efficient, but the bank can further optimize it through targeted supply chain finance solutions. Extending Supplier Finance to the company’s top 20% of payables — representing approximately RMB8.7 billion of the RMB43.5 billion total — could allow the company to extend DPO by an additional 10-15 days without straining supplier relationships. This would improve CCC to approximately -35 days, freeing up an estimated RMB4-6 billion in additional working capital.

For the company’s overseas dealer network, Distributor Finance can support working capital for dealers in Thailand, Malaysia, and Vietnam, where local financing may be less accessible. This would not directly impact the company’s balance sheet but would strengthen its distribution channel and support sales growth. Deep-tier financial supply chain management could be explored for the company’s Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers in Thailand, where smaller suppliers may face financing constraints that could disrupt production.

8.2 Treasury Pooling, Sustainability & Transition Finance Triggers

The company’s multi-entity structure in Thailand (seven subsidiaries) and planned entities in Malaysia and Vietnam creates a clear opportunity for notional pooling across ASEAN. The bank can implement a cross-border cash sweep structure that centralizes surplus liquidity from Thailand operations into a regional treasury hub, potentially in Singapore, reducing idle cash and optimizing interest income. The Hong Kong entities (Tide Technology, HWA Ding) could serve as an additional liquidity hub for China-ASEAN flows.

On sustainability, DemoMotor’s new energy transition is still in its early stages — the company has not disclosed specific decarbonization targets or green financing frameworks. However, the automotive industry is under increasing regulatory pressure to reduce emissions, and the company’s EV penetration rate of 43.99% in domestic passenger vehicles provides a strong baseline for a sustainability-linked loan structure. The bank can propose a facility with KPIs tied to: (a) EV sales mix target (e.g., 60% by 2027), (b) manufacturing carbon intensity reduction (e.g., 20% reduction by 2028), or (c) supply chain sustainability certification for Thai suppliers. This would align with the bank’s sustainable finance portfolio target of 25% of wholesale portfolio by 2026 and its net zero commitment by 2050.

The company’s expansion into Malaysia and Vietnam also presents an opportunity for transition finance under the bank’s framework, particularly if the CKD plants incorporate green manufacturing processes or renewable energy sources. The bank’s U-Build program could support green factory construction in these markets.
